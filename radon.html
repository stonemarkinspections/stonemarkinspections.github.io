Why Radon Testing Matters

Radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States and the leading cause among non-smokers. Elevated radon levels have been found in homes of all ages, styles, and price ranges — including new construction.

Because radon cannot be seen or smelled, testing is the only reliable way to know whether a home has elevated levels.

Professional vs DIY Radon Tests

While consumer radon test kits are widely available, real estate transactions and risk decisions often require professionally conducted and documented measurements.

  • Use of calibrated, approved continuous radon monitors
  • Controlled test conditions verified and documented
  • Chain-of-custody and tamper-resistant devices
  • Clear reporting suitable for real estate decisions
  • Interpretation of results and next-step guidance
A professional radon test provides more than a number — it provides confidence in how that number was obtained.

How the Radon Test Is Performed

  • Device placement in the lowest livable level of the home
  • Minimum 48-hour testing period under closed-house conditions
  • Continuous monitoring for spikes or interference
  • Results reviewed and documented in a clear report

When to Test for Radon

  • During a home purchase or sale
  • Before finishing a basement or lower level
  • After major renovations or HVAC changes
  • If the home has never been tested
