What a Pool & Spa Inspection Covers

Pools are complex systems—pumps, filters, heaters, plumbing, bonding, controls, and safety components. A proper inspection evaluates operation, visible condition, and common failure points.

  • Equipment condition and basic operation (pump, filter, valves, controls)
  • Visible plumbing and fittings for leaks, condition, and obvious concerns
  • Skimmers, returns, drains (visual check), and waterline components
  • Heater presence/condition (if installed) and visible venting basics
  • Safety items: barriers, gates, latches, covers (as applicable)
  • Deck condition, trip hazards, coping/edge conditions (visual)
  • Spa: circulation components, jets, controls, visible condition

Why This Matters in a Real Estate Purchase

A pool can be a value-add—or a hidden liability. Repairs can run from minor service items to major equipment replacement. A pool/spa inspection provides documentation so you can negotiate repairs, plan upgrades, or decide whether the risk is acceptable.

If you’re buying a home with a pool, a separate pool/spa inspection is strongly recommended. Most standard home inspections only include limited observations of pool components.

What This Inspection Does Not Include

This service is a visual and operational evaluation of accessible components at the time of inspection. It does not include destructive testing, pressure testing of underground lines, or disassembly of equipment.

  • No underground line pressure testing (unless separately quoted)
  • No disassembly of pumps/filters/heaters beyond normal access panels
  • No guarantee of future performance or remaining equipment life
  • No verification of permitting history or code compliance beyond visible safety concerns
